What changes

Three things are true by the end of the first quarter.

01

Nobody is the bottleneck anymore

The question that used to route to one senior engineer gets answered by the record instead — with the thread, the ticket and the change it came from attached. They get their week back.

02

Leaving stops being a loss event

Notice periods turn into handovers instead of archaeology. The decisions, the caveats and the reasoning are already recorded, because they were captured the day they happened.

03

Your agents compound instead of repeating

Every debugging session leaves something behind. The next agent — and the next engineer — starts from what was already learned rather than from nothing.

What we haven't proven yet.

Known limits
  • The server is closed source. The client inside your network is MIT and auditable; the server isn't published. We targeted the first half of 2026, missed it, and won't name a new date until we're certain.
  • We publish no measured accuracy figure. Grounding is measured internally, but nothing goes on this page until it's measured on real customer data — with its methodology.
  • Learning is optional and off by default. It needs real feedback volume and some compute before it's worth switching on. Everything else works without it.
  • Self-hosting isn't unique to us. Open-source alternatives exist and several are excellent at retrieval. What they don't do is capture what was never written down.
